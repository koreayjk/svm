For host fields · missionaries and local churches

Come as you are. Everything is settled before anyone leaves.

SVM sends college students to the mission field every school break. We do it without asking your field for money, special food or a special place to stay. This page explains why we do it this way, and how we plan each team with you.

How it works Talk with us
Why we do this

Sending teams is expensive. Students have no money. We still want to send them every break.

01

The usual way costs too much

Flights, rooms, meals and program fees add up fast. Often the sending side raises a large sum, or the field ends up carrying part of the cost.

02

Students have time, not money

American college students have long breaks and a real desire to serve. What they do not have is a budget. If a trip is expensive, most of them can never go.

03

We want to send every break

Summer, winter and spring, again and again, not one trip a year. That only works if no one is burdened: not the students beyond their flight, and not you.

A mission trip should open doors, not drain the people who open them.

Our promise to your field

You carry no cost, and no surprises.

You pay nothing

Students pay for their own round-trip flight and travel insurance. There are no fees to SVM and nothing for you to cover.

Nothing special to prepare

No special meals, no nicer room. Students live the way you live, serve alongside you, and share the gospel. You decide how food and housing work (see below).

One person to talk to

Each country has an SVM leader who cares for the students and speaks with you. Students do not contact you on their own. If a meeting helps, the leader joins it.

Everything agreed first

We talk through every detail with you before a team is approved to go: the role, dates, food, housing, safety and contacts. Our goal is that nothing is left to figure out on arrival.

What students can do

There is no fixed program. We decide the role together.

We build each team around what you need and what you can carry. These are the most common starting points. You can pick one, combine them, or tell us something else.

Teach English

Native English speakers can run classes for children, youth and adults, English camps, and conversation practice. It is a natural way to meet people and build trust.

A good fit when you have an English class, a school connection, or want to start one.

Build and repair

Extra hands for a building project: a church or classroom, painting, roofing, repairs, a clean-up. Students' experience varies, and we ask about it first.

A good fit when you have a project and not enough people to do the work.

Share the gospel and disciple

Evangelism, prayer, visiting homes, friendship and discipleship, side by side with your people. Students train weekly and learn the core gospel message in your language.

A good fit when you want more voices and more feet for outreach.

Join what you already do

Kids' programs and VBS, music, sports, media, office help, community work. Students simply serve inside your existing ministry.

A good fit when your ministry is running and you want help, not a new program.

Something we have not listed

Tell us your real need. We would rather ask a question than guess. Maximum possibility is the point: whatever fits your field and the students' gifts, we will talk it through.

Food and housing

You choose. Simple is always fine.

We never expect more than you already live with. Any of these works, and you can mix them:

Live as you liveThey eat what you eat and stay where you can host: a church floor, a shared room, a homestay.
Students handle their own mealsThey buy and cook their own food. You only point them to a market.
Students find their own placeThey arrange a simple room nearby. You only advise on a safe area.
A mixFor example, they sleep at the church and make their own meals.

Saying “please have them take care of their own food” is a normal, welcome answer. It is exactly what lets many fields say yes to more students.

Before anyone leaves

We talk through all of this with you first.

Our goal is simple: every question is answered before departure. Your SVM country leader goes through these with you.

  • The roleWhat the students will do each day and week.
  • Dates and team sizeWhich seasons suit you, how long, and how many students your field can welcome.
  • Food and housingWhich option above you prefer, and anything students should know.
  • Getting aroundAirport pickup, local transport, and how far the work is from where they stay.
  • Culture and safetyDress, manners, places to avoid, and rules for a sensitive country.
  • Who to callOne SVM leader for you, and what to do if something goes wrong.
  • What students coverTheir flight, insurance and personal spending. Written down so there is no doubt.
  • What you do not have to provideEverything you tell us you cannot or would rather not carry.

A one-page plan, agreed by both sides. We write the answers into a short plan: the role, dates, team size, food, housing, transport, safety, contacts, and what the field does not have to provide. We do not approve a team to go until you and SVM agree on it.

How it works

From first hello to a team on your field

  1. Say helloTell your SVM country leader about your ministry.
  2. Talk it throughNeeds, what you can and cannot carry, seasons, ideas. No pressure.
  3. Write the planOne page. You and SVM agree on it.
  4. Students prepareThey train, learn your language, buy travel insurance and apply for your plan.
  5. Pre-departure callThe leader goes over the plan with you once more. Students join if it helps.
  6. Go and report backThe leader stays in touch while they are with you. Students share what happened.

You get a place in the SVM app. Post news, photos and videos from your country, update what visitors should expect, and talk with your SVM country leader. Students see your news and pray for you.

Saying “not yet” is okay. If your season is wrong or the timing is hard, tell us. We would rather wait than send a team that adds weight to your field.

We protect your field. Students are told never to post local believers' faces, real names or exact church locations. Testimonies and photos are reviewed before they appear, and sensitive countries hide names and limit photos.
Questions hosts ask

Straight answers

Do we have to pay anything?

No. Students pay their own flights and travel insurance. There are no fees to SVM and no program costs for your field.

We have very little space. Can we still host?

Yes. Ask for a small team, a shorter stay, or have students find their own simple room. Tell your country leader what you can do, and we plan around it.

How many students, and for how long?

You decide with your country leader. Stays are usually a month or longer, in the seasons that suit you. We start small if you prefer.

Who is responsible for the students?

SVM trains and screens them, requires travel insurance, and an SVM country leader looks after them. Students check in with the leader during the trip. You are never asked to supervise them alone.

Can we choose who comes?

Your leader talks with you about the skills and character that fit your plan. Students then apply for that specific plan, and the leader approves the team.

What if something is not working?

Tell your country leader right away. You never have to handle it alone, and we adjust the plan together.
